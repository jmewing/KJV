# The Merneptah Stele ("Israel Stele")

**Source:** Wikipedia (summarized from multiple scholarly citations)  
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merneptah_Stele

## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Merneptah Stele (also called the Israel Stele or Victory Stele of Merneptah)
- **Date:** c. 1208 B.C.E. (5th year of Pharaoh Merneptah, 19th Dynasty)
- **Material:** Black granite
- **Discovered:** 1896 at Thebes, Egypt
- **Discoverer:** Flinders Petrie
- **Current location:** Egyptian Museum, Cairo
- **Language:** Ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs
- **Biblical connection:** Earliest known extra-biblical mention of "Israel" (Exodus / Conquest period traditions)

## Key Points

- The stele is primarily a victory inscription celebrating Merneptah’s defeat of Libyan invaders and their Sea Peoples allies.
- Its final lines describe a separate campaign in Canaan, listing defeated peoples and cities: Ashkelon, Gezer, Yenoam, and Israel.
- The hieroglyphic determinative used for "Israel" (a people, not a city-state) is significant: it depicts Israel as a tribal/nomadic group rather than a settled kingdom at this early date.
- The relevant lines read:
  > "Israel is laid waste—its seed is no more; Hurru has become a widow because of Egypt."
- It is one of only four known Iron Age inscriptions that mention ancient Israel by name, alongside the Mesha Stele, Tel Dan Stele, and Kurkh Monoliths.

## What It Corroborates

- A people called Israel existed in Canaan by the late 13th century B.C.E.
- Egypt’s military campaigns in Canaan align broadly with the period in which the Exodus and Conquest traditions are set.
- Israel was already recognized as a distinct people/entity by a major foreign power before the rise of the Israelite monarchy.

## Status

The Merneptah Stele is widely accepted as the earliest extra-biblical reference to Israel. Its exact relationship to the biblical Exodus and Conquest narratives remains debated, but it firmly places a group called "Israel" in Canaan around 1200 B.C.E.
