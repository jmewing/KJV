# Solomon’s First Temple — Archaeology of the House of God

## Artifact Summary

**Solomon’s First Temple** was built in Jerusalem around the mid-10th century B.C.E. as a permanent house of worship for the God of Israel. The biblical account in 1 Kings 6–8 describes its dimensions, materials, furnishings, and dedication.

While the Temple Mount itself has not been excavated due to religious and political restrictions, archaeological work in Jerusalem and surrounding areas has uncovered evidence consistent with the biblical narrative of a monumental royal building project during Solomon’s reign.

## Key Points

- The Bible describes the temple as built of costly stone, cedar from Lebanon, and overlaid with gold.
- Large stone structures and monumental building remains from the 10th century B.C.E. have been found in Jerusalem.
- Temple architecture parallels from other ancient Near Eastern sites confirm that such elaborate sacred buildings were common in the region.
- Inscriptions and seals mention temple officials, priestly families, and materials connected with temple service.

## What It Corroborates

The archaeological context supports the historicity of a grand temple complex in Jerusalem during the united monarchy, matching the biblical description of Solomon’s building program.

## KJV Connections

- 1 Kings 6:1–38 details the construction of Solomon’s Temple.
- 2 Chronicles 3–5 describes the furnishings, including the altar, the molten sea, and the cherubim.
- Psalm 122:1 expresses the joy of going up to the house of the Lord.

## Application

Solomon’s Temple reminds believers that God desired a dwelling place among His people and that worship was central to the life of Israel. It foreshadows the greater temple, Christ Himself, in whom the fullness of God dwells bodily.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Has Solomon’s Temple been found?**  
Direct excavation of the Temple Mount has not taken place, but 10th-century B.C.E. monumental construction in Jerusalem matches the biblical timeframe and scale.

**What evidence points to the temple’s existence?**  
Biblical records, ancient Near Eastern parallels, and Jerusalem archaeology all support the historicity of the First Temple.

**How does the temple relate to the KJV?**  
The King James Version preserves the detailed biblical account of the temple’s construction, purpose, and dedication.
