# The Nazareth Inscription

**Source:** Wikipedia  
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth_Inscription

## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Marble tablet with a Greek imperial edict
- **Date:** 1st half of the 1st century C.E. (based on epigraphy; debated)
- **Acquired:** 1878 in Nazareth by collector Wilhelm Fröhner
- **Current location:** Bibliothèque nationale / Cabinet des Médailles, Louvre, Paris
- **Language:** Koine Greek
- **Biblical connection:** Tomb disturbance / body theft; sometimes discussed in relation to reports of Jesus’ empty tomb

## Key Points

- The edict orders capital punishment for anyone who disturbs graves, moves buried bodies, or removes sealing stones from tombs.
- Translation highlights:
  > "It is my decision [concerning] graves and tombs... You are absolutely not to allow anyone to move [those who have been entombed]. But if [someone does], I wish that [violator] to suffer capital punishment under the title of tomb-breaker."
- The marble’s isotopic signature (2020 study in *Journal of Archaeological Science*) points to a quarry on the Greek island of Kos, not Nazareth, weakening the theory that it was issued in direct response to Jesus’ resurrection.
- An alternative proposal links it to the desecration of the tomb of the Kos tyrant Nikias around 20 B.C.E.

## What It Corroborates

- Roman imperial concern with tomb violation and proper burial was real and legally formalized in the early 1st century C.E.
- The cultural setting of the Gospels — anxiety over tomb disturbance, sealed tombs, and capital penalties — matches known Roman law and practice.
- The inscription is consistent with the general historical plausibility of the Gospel tomb narratives, even though its direct connection to Jesus is uncertain.

## Status

The Nazareth Inscription is interesting but not direct evidence for the resurrection. Its provenance is disputed, and the 2020 isotope study strongly suggests a Kos origin. It remains a useful example of Roman burial law rather than proof of a specific biblical event.
