# Solomonic Gates at Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer

## What They Are

The "Solomonic gates" are monumental **six-chambered city gates** excavated at three major sites in Israel:

- **Tel Hazor** (upper city)
- **Tel Megiddo** (northern gate complex)
- **Tel Gezer** (western gate)

These gates share a common architectural plan: an entrance flanked by **two towers**, with **three chambers on each side**, creating a symmetrical six-chambered layout.

## Why They Matter

The biblical text records that **King Solomon** built or rebuilt fortifications at these three cities:

> *"And this is the reason of the levy which king Solomon raised; for to build the house of the LORD, and his own house, and Millo, and the wall of Jerusalem, and Hazor, and Megiddo, and Gezer."* — 1 Kings 9:15 (KJV)

- The similarity in design suggests a single royal building program spanning strategic cities.
- Yigael Yadin, excavator of Hazor, first proposed identifying these gates as Solomonic in the 1950s–1960s.
- The gates demonstrate centralized state planning and military engineering in the early Israelite monarchy.

## Dating Debate

- **Traditional view (maximalist):** The gates were built in the **10th century B.C.E.** during Solomon’s reign, matching 1 Kings 9:15.
- **Revisionist view (minimalist / low chronology):** Some scholars, including David Ussishkin, argue that the Megiddo gate may belong to the **9th century B.C.E.**, built by kings such as **Ahab** or **Jeroboam II**. Renewed excavations at Gezer have also questioned whether the gate should automatically be labeled "Solomonic."
- Archaeologists at Gezer (Steve Ortiz and Sam Wolff) have deliberately avoided assuming the Solomonic attribution and are re-examining the stratigraphy.

## KJV Connection

> *"And Solomon gathered together chariots and horsemen: and he had a thousand and four hundred chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he bestowed in the cities for chariots, and with the king at Jerusalem... And he built... Hazor, and Megiddo, and Gezer."* — 1 Kings 9:19–24 (KJV, condensed)

- The KJV reflects the Hebrew account of Solomon’s royal building program, including the fortification of these three strategic sites.
- The gates provide possible physical evidence of that program, though their exact date remains contested.

## Notes

- The identification is not universally accepted. Some archaeologists distinguish between "six-chambered gate" as an architectural description and "Solomonic gate" as a historical claim.
- Similar six-chambered gates have been found at **Lachish** and other sites, complicating a simple Solomonic-only attribution.
- This entry is best used as a case study in how archaeology and biblical history interact, especially where dating is debated.
