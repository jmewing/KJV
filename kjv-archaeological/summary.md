## Quick Reference: Key Findings

### 1. Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone)

- **What it is:** A 9th-century B.C.E. basalt monument from Moab (modern Jordan) celebrating King Mesha's rebellion against Israel.
- **Why it matters:** For decades scholars read line 31 as mentioning the "House of David." A 2019/2024 re-examination proposes the name **Balak**, the Moabite king in the Balaam story.
- **Status:** Active scholarly debate; read both the traditional and new interpretations.
- **KJV connection:** Numbers 22–24; 2 Kings 3:4–27.

### 2. Tel Dan Stele ("House of David")

- **What it is:** A fragmentary Aramean victory inscription from the 9th century B.C.E.
- **Why it matters:** It refers to the **"king of the House of David"** and the "king of Israel" — the earliest widely accepted extra-biblical mention of David and his dynasty.
- **KJV connection:** 2 Kings 8–9; 2 Chronicles 22.

### 3. Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaa)

- **What it is:** The largest and most complete Dead Sea Scroll, containing the entire Book of Isaiah.
- **Why it matters:** It is about **1,000 years older** than the next oldest complete Hebrew Isaiah manuscripts. Its text closely matches the medieval Masoretic Text that underlies the KJV.
- **KJV connection:** Isaiah (all 66 chapters); textual reliability of the Old Testament.

### 4. Siloam Inscription

- **What it is:** A Hebrew inscription carved inside the tunnel that brought water from the Gihon Spring to the Pool of Siloam.
- **Why it matters:** It records the moment two digging crews met underground, matching the biblical description of Hezekiah's tunnel construction.
- **KJV connection:** 2 Kings 20:20; 2 Chronicles 32:1–4, 30.

### 5. Merneptah Stele ("Israel Stele")

- **What it is:** A 3-meter granite victory stele of Pharaoh Merneptah from c. 1208 B.C.E.
- **Why it matters:** It contains the earliest known extra-biblical mention of "Israel" as a people in Canaan.
- **KJV connection:** Exodus / Numbers / Joshua (Israel’s presence in Canaan).

### 6. Pontius Pilate Inscription (Pilate Stone)

- **What it is:** A 1st-century Latin building dedication from Caesarea Maritima.
- **Why it matters:** It names "Pontius Pilate, Prefect of Judea" — the only contemporary archaeological inscription to do so.
- **KJV connection:** Matthew 27; Mark 15; Luke 23; John 18–19.

### 7. Caiaphas Ossuary

- **What it is:** A decorated 1st-century bone box inscribed "Joseph son of Caiaphas."
- **Why it matters:** It provides archaeological attestation for the high priest Caiaphas, a central figure in Jesus’ trial.
- **KJV connection:** Matthew 26; John 18; Acts 4:6.

### 8. Nazareth Inscription

- **What it is:** A Greek imperial edict against tomb disturbance, dated by epigraphy to the 1st century C.E.
- **Why it matters:** It shows Roman concern with tomb violation; direct connection to Jesus’ empty tomb is uncertain.
- **KJV connection:** Matthew 27:62–66; 28:11–15.

### 9. Kurkh Monoliths

- **What it is:** Two Assyrian stelae recording campaigns of Shalmaneser III (c. 852 B.C.E.).
- **Why it matters:** The Battle of Qarqar account names "Ahab of Israel" and his military contribution.
- **KJV connection:** 1 Kings 16–22.

### 10. Sennacherib's Annals (Taylor Prism)

- **What it is:** Hexagonal clay prisms inscribed with Sennacherib’s account of his Judah campaign.
- **Why it matters:** Names "Hezekiah of Judah" and "Jerusalem" in cuneiform, matching the biblical siege narrative.
- **KJV connection:** 2 Kings 18–19; Isaiah 36–37; 2 Chronicles 32.

### 11. Lachish Reliefs

- **What it is:** Assyrian palace reliefs depicting the siege and capture of Lachish.
- **Why it matters:** The most elaborate visual record of a specific biblical siege, confirmed by excavations at Tel Lachish.
- **KJV connection:** 2 Kings 18:13–17; 2 Chronicles 32:9; Isaiah 36:1–2.

### 12. King Hezekiah Bulla

- **What it is:** A clay seal impression reading "Belonging to Hezekiah, [son of] Ahaz, king of Judah."
- **Why it matters:** The first royal seal of an Israelite/Judean king found in a scientific excavation.
- **KJV connection:** 2 Kings 18–20; Isaiah 36–39; 2 Chronicles 29–32.

### 13. Jericho Destruction

- **What it is:** Archaeological evidence for the violent destruction of Bronze Age Jericho.
- **Why it matters:** The profile (fallen walls, burning, abandonment) is consistent with Joshua 6, though the date is debated.
- **KJV connection:** Joshua 2; Joshua 6.
- **Status:** Sources were blocked during collection; this entry is intentionally provisional.

### 14. Delphi / Gallio Inscription

- **What it is:** A Greek inscription from Delphi in which Emperor Claudius names **Lucius Junius Gallio, proconsul of Achaia**.
- **Why it matters:** It confirms Gallio’s governorship and helps date Paul’s ministry in Corinth to about **50–52 C.E.** (Acts 18:12–17).
- **KJV connection:** Acts 18 — Paul’s appearance before Gallio.

### 15. Erastus Inscription (Corinth)

- **What it is:** A Latin pavement dedication by **Erastus**, an aedile (city official) at Corinth.
- **Why it matters:** It may be the same **Erastus the chamberlain of the city** greeted by Paul in Romans 16:23.
- **Status:** The identification is debated, but the name is rare in Corinthian inscriptions.
- **KJV connection:** Romans 16:23; Acts 19:22; 2 Timothy 4:20.

### 16. Machaerus

- **What it is:** A Herodian fortress east of the Dead Sea where Josephus says John the Baptist was imprisoned and executed.
- **Why it matters:** Excavations have uncovered a first-century palace, bathhouse, and courtyard at the site.
- **KJV connection:** Matthew 14:3–10; Mark 6:14–29.

### 17. Capernaum

- **What it is:** A first-century fishing village on the Sea of Galilee, identified as Jesus’ base of ministry after Nazareth.
- **Why it matters:** Excavations have revealed a first-century synagogue and the venerated “House of Peter.”
- **KJV connection:** Matthew 4:13; Matthew 17:24; Mark 1:21; John 6.

### 18. Magdala Synagogue

- **What it is:** The remains of a Second Temple-period synagogue at Magdala/Migdal on the Sea of Galilee.
- **Why it matters:** It is one of the oldest securely dated Galilean synagogues and was in use during Jesus’ lifetime.
- **KJV connection:** Luke 8:2; Mark 16:9 — Mary Magdalene.

### 19. Caesarea Maritima

- **What it is:** Herod the Great’s port city and the Roman capital of Judea, residence of governors including Pontius Pilate.
- **Why it matters:** The site’s theater, palace, harbor, and inscriptions match Luke’s detailed account of Paul’s imprisonment and hearings before Felix and Festus.
- **KJV connection:** Acts 8:40; 10; 21:8; 23–26.

### 20. Papyrus 52 and Papyrus 75

- **What it is:** Two of the earliest surviving Greek New Testament papyri (John and Luke).
- **Why it matters:** 𝔓52 shows the Gospel of John was copied early; 𝔓75 shows the text was already stable close to Codex Vaticanus.
- **KJV connection:** Supports the antiquity and careful transmission of the New Testament manuscript tradition underlying English translations.
- **Status:** Paleographic dating is debated; ranges rather than exact dates are given.

### 21. Solomonic Gates (Hazor, Megiddo, Gezer)

- **What it is:** Monumental six-chambered gates at three major Israelite cities.
- **Why it matters:** Their shared architectural plan suggests a centralized royal building program matching **1 Kings 9:15**.
- **Status:** The Solomonic date is debated; some scholars assign the gates to the 9th century B.C.E. (Ahab / Jeroboam II).
- **KJV connection:** 1 Kings 9:15–19.

### 22. Tel Arad Ostraca

- **What it is:** Judahite military correspondence from a 7th-century B.C.E. fortress archive.
- **Why it matters:** One ostracon mentions the **"House of God" / "House of YHWH"**, likely the Jerusalem Temple, in everyday administrative writing.
- **KJV connection:** 1 Kings 6:1; the phrase "house of the LORD" throughout the KJV.

### 23. Sea of Galilee Boat

- **What it is:** A 1st-century B.C.E.–1st-century C.E. fishing boat recovered from the Kinneret mud in 1986.
- **Why it matters:** It is the only surviving boat from the time and place of Jesus’ Galilean ministry, matching Gospel descriptions of boat travel and fishing.
- **KJV connection:** Luke 5:2–3; Mark 4:35–36; Matthew 14:34.

### 24. Dead Sea Scrolls

- **What it is:** Biblical and sectarian manuscripts from Qumran, dating from the 3rd century B.C.E. to the 1st century C.E.
- **Why it matters:** The biblical scrolls are nearly identical to the later Masoretic Text used by the KJV translators, confirming careful textual transmission.
- **KJV connection:** Isaiah, Psalms, Deuteronomy, and every other OT book except Esther.

### 25. Solomon’s First Temple

- **What it is:** The temple built by Solomon in Jerusalem, described in detail in 1 Kings 6–8.
- **Why it matters:** 10th-century B.C.E. monumental building remains in Jerusalem match the biblical timeframe and scale of Solomon’s royal projects.
- **KJV connection:** 1 Kings 6:1–38; 2 Chronicles 3–5; Psalm 122:1.

### 26. Sodom and Gomorrah

- **What it is:** The cities of the plain destroyed by fire from heaven in Genesis 19.
- **Why it matters:** Archaeological destruction layers near the Dead Sea, especially at Tall el-Hammam, show evidence of a sudden, high-temperature catastrophic event.
- **KJV connection:** Genesis 19:24–25; Deuteronomy 29:23; 2 Peter 2:6.

### 27. Pools of Bethesda and Siloam

- **What it is:** Two Jerusalem pools connected with Jesus’ miracles in John’s Gospel.
- **Why it matters:** Excavations have identified both sites with features matching the Gospel descriptions: five porches at Bethesda and a Second Temple pool at Siloam.
- **KJV connection:** John 5:2; John 9:6–7.

### 28. Temple Mount / Western Wall

- **What it is:** The sacred platform where Solomon and Herod built their temples, and the surviving Herodian retaining wall known as the Western Wall.
- **Why it matters:** The massive stones and nearby Second Temple remains confirm the grandeur of the temple complex central to Jewish worship and the Gospels.
- **KJV connection:** 2 Chronicles 3:1; Matthew 24:1–2; John 2:19–21.

## Notes for Your Research

- Treat Wikipedia as a starting point, not a final authority. Follow its citations into peer-reviewed journals, museum catalogs, and primary excavation reports.
- For controversial finds (Jericho chronology, Balak reading, Nazareth Inscription provenance), read both sides — maximalist/minimalist, evangelical/secular — and weigh the evidence.
- The KJV New Testament relies on later manuscripts (Textus Receptus / Byzantine tradition). For textual reliability questions, pair the Dead Sea Scrolls with studies of New Testament papyri (e.g., P52, P75, Codex Sinaiticus/Vaticanus).
