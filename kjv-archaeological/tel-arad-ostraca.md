# Tel Arad Ostraca

## What They Are

The **Arad ostraca** are more than 200 Hebrew inscriptions on pottery sherds discovered at **Tel Arad**, a fortified city in the Negev desert of southern Israel. Most come from a single archive dated to the late **7th century B.C.E.** (roughly 600 B.C.E.), during the final decades of the Kingdom of Judah.

## Why They Matter

- The ostraca are Judahite military correspondence, mostly addressed to a quartermaster or commander named **Eliashib**.
- They mention supplies (wine, oil, flour), troop movements, and place names.
- One ostracon, the **"House of God" / "House of YHWH"** ostracon, contains a phrase that may refer to the Jerusalem Temple.
- They provide rare primary evidence of Hebrew literacy, administration, and religious language just before the Babylonian exile.

## The "House of God" Ostracon

The most famous Arad ostracon reads (in translation):

> *"To my lord Eliashib, may the LORD seek your welfare. And now, give to Shemaryahu a lethech and to the Kerosi give a homer. And as to the matter which you commanded me — it is well; he is in the House of God."*

- The phrase **"House of God"** (Hebrew **בית יהוה**, *Beit YHWH*) is understood by many scholars as a reference to the Temple in Jerusalem.
- It shows that Temple-related correspondence could reach a remote fortress, supporting the centrality of the Jerusalem Temple in late Judahite administration and religion.
- Recent multispectral imaging in 2017 revealed additional text on ostraca previously thought blank, showing the archive still holds unread information.

## KJV Connection

> *"And it came to pass in the four hundred and fourscore year after the children of Israel were come out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel, in the month Zif, which is the second month, that he began to build the house of the LORD."* — 1 Kings 6:1 (KJV)

- The KJV consistently calls the Jerusalem Temple the **"house of the LORD"** (Hebrew *Beit YHWH*), the same phrase found on the Arad ostracon.
- The ostraca confirm that ordinary Judahite officials used this exact theological vocabulary in everyday correspondence.
- They also reflect the kind of fortified border administration described in passages such as **2 Chronicles 17:12–19** (Jehoshaphat’s garrisons) and the military correspondence of the late monarchy.

## Notes

- The exact reading of "House of God" is debated; some scholars suggest it could refer to a local shrine rather than the Jerusalem Temple. The majority view favors the Jerusalem Temple.
- The ostraca were written in a cursive Hebrew script close to that of the later Dead Sea Scrolls and the Masoretic Text.
