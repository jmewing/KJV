# Capernaum — Jesus’ Ministry Base in Galilee

## What It Is

**Capernaum** (Hebrew: Kfar Naḥum, “Nahum’s village”) was a fishing village on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee. Archaeological work has uncovered houses, a synagogue, an octagonal church, streets, olive presses, and fishing equipment spanning the Hasmonean through Byzantine periods.

## Why It Matters

- The Gospels identify Capernaum as the center of Jesus’ public ministry in Galilee after he left Nazareth.
- Excavations revealed a first-century house believed by many to be the home of **Simon Peter**, later transformed into a domus-ecclesia (house-church) and then a fifth-century octagonal church.
- The site confirms the material culture of a first-century Jewish fishing village: stone vessels, mills, fish hooks, and weights — consistent with Gospel descriptions.

## The Site

- Inhabited from the 2nd century B.C.E. through the 11th century C.E.
- A later (4th–5th century) synagogue stands on basalt foundations that may belong to a **first-century synagogue** in which Jesus taught.
- The “House of Peter” shows layers of veneration from the first century onward, including Christian graffiti and a church built over it.

## KJV Connection

> *“And leaving Nazareth, he came and dwelt in Capernaum, which is upon the sea coast, in the borders of Zabulon and Nephthalim...”* — Matthew 4:13 (KJV)

> *“And when they were come to Capernaum, they that received tribute money came to Peter...”* — Matthew 17:24 (KJV)

- Capernaum is cited in all four Gospels as the site of healings, teachings, and the calling of disciples.
- The Gospel references to a synagogue, a tax collector (Matthew), a Roman centurion, and fishing gear all match the archaeological record.

## Notes

- The visible white limestone synagogue dates to the 4th–5th century; its first-century predecessor lies beneath it.
- The identification of the “House of Peter” is based on early Christian tradition and archaeological evidence, not on an explicit inscription.
