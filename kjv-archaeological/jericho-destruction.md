## What the Scholarly Discussion Is About

- The destruction of Bronze Age Jericho is one of the most debated topics in biblical archaeology.
- Kathleen Kenyon’s mid-20th-century excavations dated the final destruction of Jericho’s fortified city (City IV) to c. 1550 B.C.E., well before the traditional Israelite conquest date.
- Bryant Wood and, more recently, Douglas Petrovich and others have challenged the dating, arguing that the destruction evidence (fallen walls, burned city, stored grain) better fits a Late Bronze Age conquest around 1400 B.C.E.
- The 2023 MDPI article by Kennedy, “The Bronze Age Destruction of Jericho, Archaeology, and the Book of Joshua,” reviews the evidence and argues for re-evaluating the chronology in light of radiocarbon and stratigraphic data.

## Key Points (from available summaries)

- Jericho (Tell es-Sultan) shows evidence of a heavily fortified Bronze Age city with massive walls.
- A destruction layer includes collapsed mudbrick walls and extensive burning.
- Some researchers connect this to Joshua 6 (walls falling, city devoted to destruction).
- Others argue the timing does not align with the Israelite conquest and that the biblical account reflects later memory or tradition.

## What It May Corroborate

- A major fortified Bronze Age city existed at Jericho and was violently destroyed.
- The archaeological profile (walls, fire, abandonment) is broadly consistent with the dramatic account in Joshua 6.
- The main disagreement is chronology: whether the destruction happened c. 1400 B.C.E. (early Exodus/Conquest) or c. 1550 B.C.E. (Middle Bronze Age).

## Status

This find is contested. Mainstream scholarship generally places the destruction earlier than the traditional Conquest, while maximalist and some evangelical scholars argue for a later date matching Joshua.
