# Magdala / Migdal Synagogue

## What It Is

**Magdala** (also Migdal, “Tower”) was a first-century Jewish town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, about 5 km north of Tiberias. Excavations begun in 2006 and 2009 uncovered a well-preserved **Second Temple-period synagogue** and the famous **Magdala Stone**, carved with a seven-branched menorah.

## Why It Matters

- Magdala is widely identified as the home of **Mary Magdalene** (Mary of Magdala), a follower of Jesus mentioned in all four Gospels.
- The synagogue is one of the oldest found in Galilee and one of only a few securely dated to the **Second Temple period** (before 70 C.E.).
- The **Magdala Stone**, a carved stone block found in the synagogue’s center, depicts the menorah and other Temple imagery, showing synagogue worship focused on Jerusalem and the Temple.

## The Site

- Settlement began in the Hellenistic period (2nd–1st century B.C.E.) and continued into the late Roman period.
- The first synagogue dates to approximately **50 B.C.E.–100 C.E.**, squarely within Jesus’ lifetime.
- A **second Second-Temple-period synagogue** was discovered in 2021 — the first time two such synagogues have been found at one site.
- The town was destroyed by the Romans during the First Jewish-Roman War (66–73 C.E.).

## KJV Connection

> *“Now when Jesus was risen early the first day of the week, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene, out of whom he had cast seven devils.”* — Mark 16:9 (KJV)

> *“And certain women, which had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils...”* — Luke 8:2 (KJV)

- Magdala provides the archaeological background for Mary Magdalene’s hometown.
- The synagogue shows that first-century Galilean Jews gathered in purpose-built assembly halls for prayer and Torah reading — the very setting of many Gospel scenes.

## Notes

- The exact link between Mary Magdalene and this specific town is traditional rather than explicitly stated in the New Testament.
- The Magdala Stone is the earliest known representation of a seven-branched menorah from a synagogue context outside Jerusalem.
