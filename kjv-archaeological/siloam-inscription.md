# The Siloam (Hezekiah's Tunnel) Inscription

**Sources:** Center for Online Judaic Studies + Wikipedia  
**URLs:**
- https://cojs.org/hezekiah-s_-or_siloam-_tunnel_inscription-_701_bce/
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siloam_inscription

## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Siloam Inscription (KAI 189)
- **Date:** c. 700 B.C.E.
- **Discovered:** 1880 by Jacob Eliahu (a student of Conrad Schick)
- **Current location:** Istanbul Archaeology Museums, Turkey
- **Material:** Limestone, cut into the rock wall of the tunnel
- **Language:** Biblical Hebrew in Paleo-Hebrew script
- **Biblical connection:** 2 Kings 20:20; 2 Chronicles 32:1–4, 30

## Key Points

- The inscription records the moment when two digging crews, working from opposite ends, met deep underground while constructing the Siloam Tunnel.
- The tunnel channels water from the Gihon Spring to the Pool of Siloam inside Jerusalem's defenses.
- It is the only known ancient Israelite/Judahite inscription commemorating a public construction project.
- Translation highlights:
  > "[The day of] the breach. This is the record of how the tunnel was breached. While [the excavators were wielding] their pickaxes, each man toward his co-worker... the excavators struck, each man to meet his co-worker, pick-axe against pick-[a]xe. Then the water flowed from the spring to the pool, a distance of one thousand and two hundred cubits."
- The tunnel itself was discovered in 1838 by Edward Robinson; the inscription was removed in 1890 and cracked in the process.

## What It Corroborates

- The historicity of King Hezekiah's waterworks project in preparation for Sennacherib's Assyrian siege (2 Kings 20:20; 2 Chronicles 32).
- Advanced Iron Age engineering and literacy in 8th-century Judah.
- The geography of Jerusalem described in the Bible (Gihon Spring, City of David, Pool of Siloam).

## Note

Some scholars debate whether the tunnel was begun by Hezekiah or by an earlier Judaean king, but the inscription's script and engineering context strongly support an 8th-century B.C.E. date consistent with Hezekiah's reign.
