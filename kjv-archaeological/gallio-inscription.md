# Gallio / Delphi Inscription

## What It Is

A Greek imperial inscription discovered at Delphi (central Greece), dating to around **52 C.E.**, in which Emperor Claudius addresses the local city and refers to **“Lucius Junius Gallio, my friend and proconsul of Achaia.”**

This is the same Gallio before whom Paul was brought by the Jews of Corinth according to **Acts 18:12–17**.

## Why It Matters

- The inscription independently corroborates the existence and office of Gallio, the Roman governor mentioned in Acts.
- Because proconsuls normally served a one-year term, the inscription helps fix the date of Paul’s Corinthian ministry.
- Acts 18 says Paul stayed in Corinth “a year and six months” and appeared before Gallio. Combining the Delphi date with Acts yields a Pauline chronology placing the Corinth mission roughly **50–52 C.E.**

## The Biblical Figure

- **Lucius Junius Gallio Annaeanus** was a Roman senator, brother of the philosopher **Seneca the Younger**, and suffect consul in the mid-50s C.E.
- In Acts he dismisses the Jewish accusation against Paul as an internal religious dispute, refusing to rule on it.

## KJV Connection

> *“And when Gallio was the deputy of Achaia, the Jews made insurrection with one accord against Paul, and brought him to the judgment seat...”* — Acts 18:12 (KJV)

- The KJV translates Gallio’s office as “deputy,” reflecting the older English term for a Roman provincial governor (proconsul).
- The Delphi inscription confirms that a Roman official named Gallio held exactly this office in Achaia at the right time.

- The inscription is one of the most important extra-biblical anchors for New Testament chronology.
