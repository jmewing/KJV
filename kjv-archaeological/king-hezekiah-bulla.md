## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Clay bulla (seal impression)
- **Date:** Reign of Hezekiah, c. 716–686 B.C.E.
- **Discovered:** 2009–2010 in the Ophel excavations, Jerusalem; announced in 2015
- **Excavator:** Dr. Eilat Mazar (Hebrew University / Israel Antiquities Authority)
- **Current location:** Israel Antiquities Authority / Israel Museum
- **Language:** Paleo-Hebrew
- **Biblical connection:** King Hezekiah son of Ahaz, king of Judah (2 Kings 18–20; Isaiah 36–39; 2 Chronicles 29–32)

## Key Points

- The bulla measures about 13 × 12 mm and reads in Paleo-Hebrew:
  > "Belonging to Hezekiah, [son of] Ahaz, king of Judah."
- The seal iconography shows a two-winged sun disk flanked by two ankh (life) symbols.
- It was found in a refuse dump next to a 10th-century B.C.E. royal/administrative building in the Ophel, along with 33 other bullae.
- Earlier Hezekiah seal impressions were known from the antiquities market, but this was the first discovered in a controlled scientific excavation.

## What It Corroborates

- Hezekiah was a real king of Judah with a functioning royal administration in Jerusalem.
- Jerusalem had a sophisticated bureaucratic system capable of sealing official documents in the late 8th century B.C.E.
- Judahite royal iconography drew on Egyptian and Assyrian motifs (ankh, winged sun), reflecting international connections.

## Status

The bulla is widely accepted as authentic. It is the first royal seal impression of an Israelite or Judean king found in a scientific excavation, making it a landmark discovery for biblical archaeology.
