# The Lachish Reliefs

**Sources:** Wikipedia + British Museum  
**URLs:**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachish_reliefs
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Lachish

## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Assyrian palace wall reliefs depicting the siege and capture of Lachish
- **Date:** Carved c. 700–681 B.C.E.
- **Created for:** South-West Palace of Sennacherib at Nineveh
- **Discovered:** 1845–1847 by Austen Henry Layard
- **Current location:** British Museum, London
- **Biblical connection:** Sennacherib’s siege of Lachish during Hezekiah’s reign (2 Kings 18:13–17; 2 Chronicles 32:9; Isaiah 36:1–2)

## Key Points

- The reliefs are the most elaborate surviving Assyrian depiction of a city siege.
- An inscription identifies the scene: "Sennacherib, the mighty king, king of Assyria, sitting on the throne of judgment before the city of Lachish."
- The panels show the Assyrian siege ramp, battering rams, defenders on the walls, prisoners impaled, families deported, and booty paraded before Sennacherib.
- Archaeological excavations at Tel Lachish (David Ussishkin, Yosef Garfinkel, et al.) have confirmed a massive siege ramp matching the one shown in the reliefs — the oldest known in the ancient Near East.
- The Lachish letters (ostraca from c. 597–587 B.C.E.) later show the city was again a Judahite military outpost before the Babylonian destruction.

## What It Corroborates

- Lachish was a major fortified city of Judah in the late 8th century B.C.E.
- Sennacherib’s campaign against Judah was real and visually documented by Assyria itself.
- The biblical sequence — Lachish besieged, Jerusalem threatened but not taken — fits both the reliefs and Sennacherib’s Annals.

## Status

The Lachish reliefs are universally accepted as genuine and are one of the strongest visual corroborations of a specific biblical event. They are often studied alongside Sennacherib’s Annals and the Siloam Inscription as a coherent group of evidence for Hezekiah’s Judah.
