# The Temple Mount and the Western Wall — Jerusalem’s Sacred Center

## Artifact Summary

The **Temple Mount** in Jerusalem is the site where Solomon built the First Temple and where the Second Temple, expanded by Herod the Great, stood in the time of Jesus. The **Western Wall** (or Wailing Wall) is a surviving section of the retaining wall that supported Herod’s massive temple platform.

Today the Western Wall is one of the most visited prayer sites in Judaism and a powerful physical reminder of the temple complex described throughout Scripture.

## Key Points

- Herod the Great enlarged the Temple Mount platform to roughly twice its original size.
- The Western Wall’s huge ashlar stones are masterpieces of ancient engineering.
- Excavations at the base of the wall have revealed street levels, shops, and mikvaot (ritual baths) from the Second Temple period.
- The temple was the center of Jewish worship, sacrifice, and pilgrimage in biblical times.

## What It Corroborates

The Temple Mount and Western Wall confirm the scale and grandeur of the Second Temple complex and its central place in first-century Jewish life.

## KJV Connections

- 2 Chronicles 3:1: Solomon began to build the temple on Mount Moriah.
- Matthew 24:1–2: Jesus foretold the temple’s destruction, “not one stone upon another.”
- Luke 2:46: The boy Jesus was found in the temple, sitting among the teachers.
- John 2:19–21: Jesus spoke of raising up the temple of His body in three days.

## Application

The Temple Mount and Western Wall call us to remember that God appointed a place for His name and that true worship is not confined to a mountain or a wall but is offered in spirit and in truth through Christ.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is the Western Wall important?**  
It is the most visible surviving structure from the Second Temple complex and has been a place of Jewish prayer for centuries.

**Can the Temple Mount be excavated?**  
Modern political and religious sensitivities prevent full excavation, but surface archaeology and nearby digs provide important context.

**Is the Temple Mount the same as Mount Moriah?**  
Yes, tradition identifies the Temple Mount with Mount Moriah, where Abraham was called to offer Isaac and where Solomon built the temple.
