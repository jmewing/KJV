# Sennacherib's Annals (Taylor Prism)

**Source:** Wikipedia + Israel Museum + Biblical Archaeology Society  
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sennacherib%27s_Annals

## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Hexagonal clay prisms inscribed with Akkadian annals
- **Date:** c. 691–689 B.C.E.
- **Created:** During the reign of Sennacherib, king of Assyria
- **Known copies:** Taylor Prism (British Museum), Chicago Prism (ISAC), Jerusalem Prism (Israel Museum)
- **Language:** Akkadian cuneiform
- **Biblical connection:** Sennacherib’s invasion of Judah and siege of Jerusalem under King Hezekiah (2 Kings 18–19; Isaiah 36–37; 2 Chronicles 32)

## Key Points

- The prisms record Sennacherib’s third campaign (701 B.C.E.) against the Levant, including his attack on Judah.
- Sennacherib claims to have conquered 46 fortified cities of Judah and deported 200,150 people.
- He says he made King Hezekiah "a prisoner in Jerusalem, his royal residence, like a bird in a cage."
- The inscription explicitly names "Hezekiah of Judah" and "Jerusalem" in cuneiform.
- Unlike the Bible, Sennacherib does not claim to have captured Jerusalem; he says Hezekiah paid tribute and submitted.

## What It Corroborates

- King Hezekiah and the city of Jerusalem were real and significant enough to be named in Assyrian royal inscriptions.
- Sennacherib’s campaign against Judah devastated the countryside, matching the biblical account of widespread destruction.
- The fact that both Assyrian and biblical sources agree on the campaign but disagree on its outcome (Jerusalem’s survival) is a classic example of how ancient historians wrote from their own perspectives.

## Status

Sennacherib’s Annals are widely accepted as authentic and are among the most important extra-biblical sources for 8th-century Judah. They corroborate the historical framework of 2 Kings 18–19 even where the details differ.
