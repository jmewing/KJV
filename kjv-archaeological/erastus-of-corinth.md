# Erastus Inscription at Corinth

## What It Is

A Latin pavement inscription found near the theater at **Corinth** reads:

> **ERASTVS PRO AEDILITATE S[VA] P[ECVNIA] STRAVIT**
> “Erastus, in return for his aedileship, laid [this pavement] at his own expense.”

The inscription was discovered in 1928/1929 and is generally dated to the **mid-1st century C.E.**, contemporary with Paul’s ministry in Corinth.

## Why It Matters

- The New Testament names an **Erastus** as “the chamberlain of the city” (KJV) or city treasurer in **Romans 16:23**, written from Corinth.
- The Corinthian Erastus inscription attests a civic official of the same name holding a financial/public-works office in the same city at the same general period.
- If the two Erasti are the same person, the inscription provides archaeological evidence for the social status of one of Paul’s associates.

## The Biblical Figure

- **Erastus** appears in the New Testament in three passages:
  - **Romans 16:23** — “Erastus the chamberlain of the city saluteth you”
  - **Acts 19:22** — Paul sends Timothy and Erastus to Macedonia
  - **2 Timothy 4:20** — Erastus remained at Corinth
- The KJV uses “chamberlain” for the Greek **οἰκονόμος** (*oikonomos*), meaning manager or treasurer.

## KJV Connection

> *“Gaius mine host, and of the whole church, saluteth you. Erastus the chamberlain of the city saluteth you, and Quartus a brother.”* — Romans 16:23 (KJV)

- Paul wrote Romans from Corinth, so the reference is to a Corinthian city official.
- The title is not identical to “aedile” (a Latin office overseeing public buildings, streets, and the marketplace), but both involve city finances and public works.

## Notes

- Some scholars question whether the inscription’s Erastus is the same man mentioned by Paul. Others note the name is rare in Corinthian inscriptions and that the chronology fits.
- The debate affects reconstructions of the social status of early Christians at Corinth.
