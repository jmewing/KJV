# The Kurkh Monoliths

**Source:** Wikipedia + British Museum  
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurkh_Monoliths

## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Two Assyrian stelae of Ashurnasirpal II (c. 879 B.C.E.) and Shalmaneser III (c. 852 B.C.E.)
- **Discovered:** 1861 at Kurkh (modern Üçtepe Höyük, Turkey) by John George Taylor
- **Current location:** British Museum, London
- **Language:** Akkadian cuneiform
- **Biblical connection:** Extra-biblical mention of King Ahab of Israel and the Battle of Qarqar (1 Kings 16–22)

## Key Points

- The Shalmaneser III monolith records the Battle of Qarqar (853 B.C.E.), where an anti-Assyrian coalition of twelve kings fought Assyria.
- The inscription names "A-ha-ab-bu Sir-ila-a-a" — Ahab the Israelite — and says he contributed 2,000 chariots and 10,000 infantry.
- This is the first extra-biblical reference to Ahab and one of only four known inscriptions from c. 1200–800 B.C.E. that mention Israel by name.
- The monoliths also contain the oldest known Assyrian reference to the Arabs.

## What It Corroborates

- The northern kingdom of Israel was a significant military power under Ahab in the mid-9th century B.C.E.
- Ahab was recognized by Assyria as a major Levantine ruler, matching the biblical portrait of a powerful but idolatrous king.
- The geopolitical situation described in 1 Kings — Israel allied with Aram/Phoenicia against larger empires — fits Assyrian records.

## Status

The identification of "Ahab of Israel" is widely though not universally accepted; some Assyriologists note that Assyrian records more commonly call the northern kingdom the "House of Omri." Even so, the monolith remains a key anchor for Israelite history in the Omride period.
