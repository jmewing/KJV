# Caesarea Maritima — Roman Capital of Judea

## What It Is

**Caesarea Maritima** was a major port city on the Mediterranean coast of Israel, built by **Herod the Great** between c. 22 and 10/9 B.C.E. and named in honor of Emperor Augustus. It served as the **capital of Roman Judea** and the residence of governors such as **Pontius Pilate**.

## Why It Matters

- Caesarea is a central setting in the **Book of Acts**: Philip the evangelist lived there (Acts 8:40; 21:8), Peter preached to Cornelius there (Acts 10), and **Paul was imprisoned there for two years** before being sent to Rome (Acts 23–26).
- Excavations have uncovered Herod’s artificial harbor (**Sebastos**), a theater, amphitheater, palace, aqueducts, temples, and inscriptions naming Roman officials.
- The **Pilate Stone** — the only known contemporary inscription naming Pontius Pilate — was found here and is already covered in `pilate-stone.md`.

## The Site

- Built on the site of Straton’s Tower, a Phoenician harbor town.
- Herod constructed one of the largest artificial harbors in the ancient Mediterranean using hydraulic concrete.
- Became the seat of Roman procurators/prefects after Judea became a province in 6 C.E.
- The city was a center of early Christianity, home to the church father **Origen** and the historian **Eusebius**.

## KJV Connection

> *“And the morrow they left the horsemen to go with him, and returned to the castle: Who, when they came to Caesarea, and delivered the epistle to the governor, presented Paul also before him.”* — Acts 23:32–33 (KJV)

> *“But when two years were fulfilled, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus; and desiring to gain favor with the Jews, Felix left Paul in bonds.”* — Acts 24:27 (ASV, parallel to KJV)

- Paul’s hearings before **Felix** and **Festus**, and his appeal to **Caesar**, took place at Caesarea (Acts 24–26).
- The city’s excavated theater, palace, and harbor match the scale and setting Luke describes.

## Notes

- Caesarea’s prominence as a Roman administrative center explains why Paul was transferred there for trial before the governor rather than being tried in Jerusalem.
- The site’s harbor engineering is considered one of Herod’s most ambitious building projects.
