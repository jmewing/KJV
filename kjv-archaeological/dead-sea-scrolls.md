# The Dead Sea Scrolls — Ancient Manuscripts and the KJV

## Artifact Summary

The **Dead Sea Scrolls** are a collection of Jewish manuscripts discovered between 1947 and 1956 in eleven caves near **Qumran**, on the northwestern shore of the Dead Sea. The scrolls date from approximately the **3rd century B.C.E. to the 1st century C.E.**

They include fragments or complete copies of every book of the Hebrew Bible except Esther, as well as sectarian writings, commentaries, and apocryphal texts.

## Key Points

- The Dead Sea Scrolls predate the oldest previously known Hebrew biblical manuscripts by nearly a thousand years.
- The biblical texts among the scrolls are remarkably consistent with the later Masoretic Text, the basis for the King James Version’s Old Testament.
- This confirms that the Scriptures were copied and transmitted with extraordinary care across many centuries.
- The scrolls also preserve ancient Hebrew spelling, grammar, and vocabulary that illuminate the KJV text.

## What It Corroborates

The Dead Sea Scrolls support the antiquity, stability, and accuracy of the biblical text. They show that the Hebrew Scriptures behind the KJV were already established and carefully preserved long before the medieval Masoretic manuscripts.

## KJV Connections

- Psalm 119 in the scrolls closely matches the Masoretic Text known to the KJV translators.
- Isaiah scrolls from Qumran are nearly identical to the Isaiah used in the KJV.
- The scrolls include copies of Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and the Minor Prophets, strengthening confidence in those KJV books.

## Application

Believers can have confidence that the Bible they read today rests on a long chain of faithful transmission. The Dead Sea Scrolls are a powerful witness that God has preserved His Word through the ages.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are the Dead Sea Scrolls different from the KJV?**  
Most biblical scrolls agree very closely with the Masoretic Text underlying the KJV. Minor spelling and wording variations exist, but no doctrinal differences.

**Why do the scrolls matter for the KJV?**  
They demonstrate that the Hebrew text behind the KJV was already standardized many centuries earlier.

**Where are the Dead Sea Scrolls now?**  
Most are housed in the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum in Jerusalem.
