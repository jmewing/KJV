# Sodom and Gomorrah — Destroyed Cities of the Plain

## Artifact Summary

**Sodom and Gomorrah** were cities of the plain east of Canaan, remembered in Genesis 19 for their wickedness and sudden destruction by fire from heaven. Archaeological interest has focused on sites around the Dead Sea that show evidence of catastrophic destruction in the Early Bronze Age.

The site of **Tall el-Hammam** in the Jordan Valley has been proposed by some researchers as a candidate for biblical Sodom, showing a massive destruction layer, melted pottery, and ash from a high-temperature event.

## Key Points

- Genesis 19 describes a sudden, fiery destruction that overthrew the cities of the plain.
- Archaeological layers at some Dead Sea sites reveal widespread destruction, burning, and abandonment.
- Tall el-Hammam’s destruction layer includes melted bricks and high-heat indicators consistent with a catastrophic event.
- The region fits the biblical description of the kikkar, the fertile “plain” where Sodom and Gomorrah were located.

## What It Corroborates

The archaeology supports the biblical claim that major cities east of the Dead Sea suffered a sudden, violent, high-temperature destruction during the Bronze Age.

## KJV Connections

- Genesis 19:24–25: “Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven.”
- Deuteronomy 29:23: The land of Sodom and Gomorrah is described as a wasteland of salt and sulfur.
- 2 Peter 2:6: Sodom and Gomorrah are given as examples of divine judgment on the ungodly.

## Application

The destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah serves as a sobering reminder of God’s hatred of sin and His justice, as well as His mercy in rescuing Lot and His people.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Has Sodom been found?**  
No site is universally accepted, but Tall el-Hammam presents a strong candidate with evidence of catastrophic destruction.

**What caused the destruction?**  
The Bible describes fire from heaven. Some researchers suggest an airburst or meteor event could account for the high-heat destruction evidence.

**Is the destruction layer dated to Abraham’s time?**  
The proposed destruction date is roughly the Middle Bronze Age, a broad period that includes the traditional patriarchal era.
