# Scholars Identify Biblical King Balak on the Mesha Stele

**Source:** Biblical Archaeology Society (Bible History Daily)  
**URL:** https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/biblical-artifacts/inscriptions/biblical-king-balak-mesha-stele/  
**Author:** Robin Ngo  
**Date:** December 05, 2024  

## Artifact Summary

- **Artifact:** Mesha Stele (also called the Moabite Stone)
- **Date:** 9th century B.C.E.
- **Material:** Black basalt, approx. 3 feet tall
- **Current location:** Louvre Museum, Paris
- **Language:** Moabite (Northwest Semitic script)
- **Biblical connection:** 2 Kings 3 (Mesha king of Moab rebels against Israel); Numbers 22–24 (Balaam and King Balak)

## Key Points

- The Mesha Stele contains a 34-line inscription in which the Moabite king Mesha celebrates his successful rebellion against Israelite rule.
- Long-time epigrapher André Lemaire had read line 31 as containing the phrase **bt[d]wd**, "House of David," which would be an early extra-biblical mention of David.
- A new study by Israel Finkelstein, Nadav Na'aman, and Thomas Römer (published in *Tel Aviv*) re-examined high-resolution images and the paper squeeze made before the stone was shattered.
- Their proposed reading of line 31 is not "House of David" but rather the personal name **Balak** — the Moabite king from the Balaam story in Numbers 22–24.
- If correct, this would be the first extra-biblical attestation of the biblical king Balak.
- The article notes that Lawrence Mykytiuk and others urge caution, so the reading remains debated.

## What It Corroborates

- The existence of a historical Moabite kingdom in the 9th century B.C.E. matching the biblical timeframe.
- The historicity of a king named Balak (if the new reading holds), previously known only from the Bible.
- The broader pattern of Israel-Moab conflict described in 2 Kings 3.

## Status

This is a recent, contested scholarly proposal, not a settled consensus. It is best read alongside the earlier "House of David" reading and the Tel Dan inscription.
